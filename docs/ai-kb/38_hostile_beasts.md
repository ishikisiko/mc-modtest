# Hostile Beasts

0.33.0 adds a framework for hostile beasts with their own model, keyframe
clips, AI, and data-driven attack moves, and its first beast,
`myvillage:demon_wolf` (妖狼). Unlike the simple fox of
[26_custom_entities.md](26_custom_entities.md), nothing is inherited from a
vanilla animal: the beast runs its own attack state machine on `Monster`,
renders through vanilla `ModelPart` and `AnimationDefinition` built from JSON
files, and needs no new dependency (no GeckoLib). The normative rules are in
[hostile-beast-runtime](../../openspec/specs/hostile-beast-runtime/spec.md);
the Entity Contract is `genops/contracts/entities/demon_wolf.yaml`.

The wolf has no natural spawning and no special drop: `/summon` and
`myvillage:demon_wolf_spawn_egg` only.

## Where each fact lives

| Fact | File | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Move timing and tuning, attributes, chase, stagger clip | `data/myvillage/beast/<name>.json`, listed in `data/myvillage/beast/index.json` | hand-edited data |
| Beast id, sounds, experience reward | `entity/beast/DemonWolfEntity.java` (a `BeastEntity` subclass) | Java |
| Hitbox, eye height, mob category, tracking range | `entity/ModEntities.java` | Java |
| Model, clips, texture, glow layer | `assets/myvillage/beast/<name>_model.json`, `<name>_animations.json`, `textures/entity/<name>/<name>.png`, `<name>_eyes.png` | generated by `tools/beastgen/defs/<name>.py` |
| Identity, intent, what is verified | `genops/contracts/entities/<name>.yaml` | contract (names fields, repeats no tuned value) |

All tuned numbers are in the data file. Docs and the contract point at it
rather than copying values.

## Server data (schema 1)

`BeastDataLoader` reads the index and one file per listed entity id from the
mod's own resources, on both sides, when attributes are registered. A missing
file, an unknown or missing field, or a broken invariant throws a
`BeastDataException` naming the file and field and stops startup; there is no
partial result. A datapack cannot override the files and `/reload` does not
re-read them.

Per move: `id`, `animation` (clip name), `total_ticks`, `windup_ticks`,
`turn_lock_tick`, `active_ticks` `[first, last]`, `immune_ticks`
`[first, last]`, `damage_multiplier`, `maximum_targets`, `use_range`
`[min, max]`, `cooldown_ticks`, `weight`, `lunge` (`tick`, `forward_min`,
`forward_max`, `up`), `hit` (`forward` `[near, far]`, `half_width`, `height`
`[low, high]`), `knockback` (`strength`, `lift`). Top level: `attributes`
(seven registered attributes), `chase` (`speed_modifier`, `move_gap_ticks`,
`cancelled_cooldown_ticks`), `stagger.animation`. Ticks are server ticks from 0
at move start. The invariants: `0 <= turn_lock_tick <= windup_ticks <=
active first <= active last < total_ticks`, `immune_ticks` inside the move,
`turn_lock_tick <= lunge.tick <= active last`, `near < far`, `low < high`,
`0 <= min <= max` for the use range, unique move ids across beasts, and clip
names that are neither `idle`/`walk`/`run` nor shared.

## Runtime

`BeastEntity extends Monster implements StaggerResistant`. Goals: float,
`BeastAttackGoal` (chase at `chase.speed_modifier`, start moves), random
stroll, look at player, look around; targets: hurt-by, nearest player in
sight. It does not despawn by distance (`removeWhenFarAway` is false); as a
`Monster` it is removed in Peaceful difficulty.

A move starts when the beast is on the ground, not stunned, sees the target,
and `BeastMoveSelector` finds a candidate: target distance (horizontal,
centre to centre) inside `use_range`, cooldown over, at least
`move_gap_ticks` since the last move ended; candidates are picked by `weight`.
The move then runs in the beast's own AI step, one move tick per step, so a
hit-stop freeze (which skips the entity's tick) pauses it. Phases
(`BeastMovePhase`): `WINDUP` before `windup_ticks`, `RELEASE` up to the first
active tick, `ACTIVE`, `RECOVERY`, `FINISHED`. Each tick it:

1. stops navigation and holds position;
2. before `turn_lock_tick`, turns toward the target (at most 30 degrees per
   tick); at `turn_lock_tick` locks the yaw and the aim point (the target's
   position then, or straight ahead at the middle of `use_range` without a
   target) and holds that yaw to the end;
3. at `lunge.tick`, applies one impulse along the locked yaw: the forward
   speed that brings the beast to rest with the aim point in the middle of the
   hit box's reach under vanilla ground and air drag (`BeastMotion`), clamped
   to `[forward_min, forward_max]`, with `up` as vertical speed;
4. on active ticks, tests the hit box: an oriented box in the locked frame
   from the feet (`BeastGeometry.hitOverlaps`, separating axes), hitting the
   target, any attackable player, or a mob targeting the beast, each once, up
   to `maximum_targets`. Damage is a mob attack of `attack_damage` times
   `damage_multiplier` (armour, shields, and enchantments apply), and the
   move's knockback along the locked yaw replaces vanilla's, scaled by the
   victim's knockback resistance;
5. ends at `total_ticks` and starts the move's cooldown.

`BeastBodyRotationControl` keeps body and head on the beast's yaw while a move
shows. Move state is transient; nothing is saved beyond vanilla `Mob` state.

## Stagger and poise (韧性)

`combat/runtime/StaggerResistant` is the only hook in the combat package; no
file under `combat/**` or `client/combat/**` names a beast.
`CombatReactionService` asks it when a combat hit lands. A target that
resists still takes the damage and the hit-stop freeze but gets no stun and no
knockback impulse. A beast resists while a move runs and its last executed
tick is inside `immune_ticks`; for the same window it carries the transient
knockback-resistance modifier `myvillage:beast_poise` (+1.0), so vanilla
knockback does not push it out of the move either. Outside the window it is
stunned like any mob: it cancels the move (cooldown
`chase.cancelled_cooldown_ticks`), shows the `stagger` clip, and may start a
move again on the first tick after the stun ends.

## Client

Synced entity data: the running move (index plus one, 0 for none), a
staggered flag, and the move tick the server last ran. One vanilla
`AnimationState` per clip name (`idle`, each move clip, the stagger clip)
follows them. Clip time counts the entity's own client ticks, so a hit-stop
pauses the clips with the body.

While a move shows, the body follows the server's path tick by tick:
`BeastEntity.lerpTo` applies each position packet in one step instead of
vanilla's three. With vanilla smoothing the drawn body trailed the server by
about two ticks of travel and the pounce's landing pose hung about 0.6 blocks
in the air (seen in game; the before stills are in
`out/preview/demon_wolf/ingame_lerp_before/`). The move clip trails the newest
synced move tick by `MOVE_CLIP_DELAY_TICKS` (2), so it shows the tick whose
position is being drawn, and is re-anchored once it drifts
`CLIP_RESYNC_TICKS` (2) from that (a hit-stop the client did not see, a late
packet). Under `/tick freeze` a pending position step is finished and the clip
shows the server's move tick exactly, so a stepped still is the pose of that
tick. The accepted cost: uneven packet arrival shows as per-tick judder during
a move; outside moves (walking, chasing) vanilla smoothing applies. No unit
test covers this path (it needs a client level); the in-game stills and the
slowed videos are its evidence. Keep the one-step lerp and the clip delay
together: changing one alone puts the pose and the drawn position out of step.

Clips are presentation only; their keys sit on the server ticks because the
generator reads them from the data file, and each move clip's length must be
`total_ticks / 20` (checked when the renderer loads and by the validator).

`BeastRenderer` serves any beast: from the id `ns:name` it reads the four
files, checks the clips against the model and the server data
(`BeastAnimationFile.check`), and draws the glow layer with
`RenderType.eyes`. The layer definition reads the model file through the
resource manager and renderers are rebuilt on every resource reload, so `F3+T`
is meant to pick up regenerated files. `BeastModel` resets the pose, turns the
model's `look.bone`, plays every started state, and drives `walk` (not
aggressive) or `run` (aggressive) through `animateWalk` unless a move or the
stagger shows; `BeastGait` derives the walk and run rates that keep planted
feet from sliding. `MyVillageClient` (client dist only) registers the renderer
and the layer; no file under `entity/**` imports client code.

## Art generator

`tools/beastgen` (standard library; previews use numpy and Pillow) owns the
four art files; never hand-edit them. `tools/beastgen/README.md` has the
modules and the loader notes.

```bash
python3 -m tools.beastgen build demon_wolf            # write the four files
python3 -m tools.beastgen build demon_wolf --check    # fail when they differ (a release-gate step)
python3 -m tools.beastgen preview demon_wolf          # out/preview/demon_wolf/
```

Move clips read their key ticks from the data file at build time, so a retune
of the ticks or of `lunge.up` needs a rebuild and nothing else. For a lunge
that leaves the ground, `quadruped.py` ports the server's `BeastMotion`
(`lunge_flight`, `move_flight`) and keys the landing pose on the touch-down
tick it computes from the data. Clips carry posture only: the server's lunge
moves the entity, so a clip adds no height or forward travel of its own. The
previews move the beast along the server's path, and a move with a jump also
gets `clip_<move>_arc.png` and `clip_<move>_landing.png`.

## Debug commands and capture

`/myvillage beast` (permission 2): `move <targets> <move_id>` starts a move on
the next AI step, ignoring range, cooldown, and gap; `status <targets>` prints
the move state (move, tick, phase, resisting, aim, cooldowns, target
distance); `debug on|off` logs move starts, ends, staggers, hits given, and
hits taken as `BEAST_DEBUG` lines in the server log. With `/tick freeze` and
`/tick step` they give single-tick stills.

`python3 -m tools.combat_capture beast` collects headless in-game evidence
into `out/preview/<name>/ingame/`, in six parts (`--parts` re-runs a subset;
details in `tools/combat_capture/README.md`):

| Part | What it records |
|---|---|
| `idle` | stills from four sides and a scale still beside the player |
| `moves` | exact-tick stills of every move (start, aim lock, lunge, first and last active, mid recovery); for a lunge that leaves the ground also the first grounded tick and two ticks later |
| `locomotion` | walk and run videos |
| `fight` | a 60 s fight (survival player, Qingfeng sword, no armour or effects, natural regeneration off, deaths by scoreboard) with the `BEAST_DEBUG` log and a per-beast table: moves started, completed, and cancelled; hits and damage on the player; player hits inside and outside immune windows and the staggers they caused; ticks from first in range to the first landed hit |
| `dodge` | slowed trials: standing still against strafing for the close-range move, and one early swing against every move |
| `slowmo` | every move at a slowed tick rate, with the server's move tick, ground flag, and position polled against video time |

It is developer evidence, never an owner verdict.

## Adding a second beast

1. A `BeastEntity` subclass in `entity/beast/` returning its id from
   `beastId()` (sounds and experience as needed).
2. Its data file `data/myvillage/beast/<name>.json` and its id in
   `index.json`.
3. Registration: `EntityType` in `ModEntities`, attributes from
   `BeastEntity.createAttributes(...)` in `ModEntityEvents`, a spawn egg in
   `ModItems` with its item model, `entity.` and `item.` names in both
   languages, a loot table, and `BeastRenderer.register` plus
   `registerLayer` in `MyVillageClient`.
4. `tools/beastgen/defs/<name>.py` plus its name in `build.DEFINITIONS`, then
   `python3 -m tools.beastgen build <name>`, and a `beastgen_check("<name>")`
   step in `tools/release_gate.py`.
5. `genops/contracts/entities/<name>.yaml` in the wolf contract's shape.
6. `python3 tools/validate_custom_entities.py`, which picks up every indexed
   beast.

Natural spawning is not part of the framework yet; the validator rejects a
beast contract that asks for it until both are extended.

## Validation

```bash
python3 tools/validate_custom_entities.py
python3 -m tools.beastgen build demon_wolf --check
/usr/bin/python3 -m unittest tools.tests.test_validate_custom_entities tools.tests.test_beastgen tools.tests.test_beast_capture
./gradlew test    # BeastDataLoaderTest, BeastMoveLogicTest, BeastGeometryTest, BeastMotionTest, BeastAssetFilesTest, CombatReactionEligibilityTest
./gradlew build
```

The validator checks, for every beast in the index: the data invariants above;
the model (bones parents first, look bone, UVs inside the texture) and clips
(channels on real bones, `idle`/`walk`/`run` looping, the stagger and every
move clip present, not looping, and `total_ticks / 20` long); texture and
glow sizes equal the model's atlas and the glow layer is partly transparent;
the generator definition exists; names in both languages; spawn-egg model and
colours; loot table; Java registration against the contract (size, eye
height, tracking range, category); renderer registration in the client class;
no client import under `entity/**`; no beast name in the combat packages; and
no spawn placement, biome modifier, or spawn biome tag.

## Verified and not verified

Automated checks and headless capture are implementation evidence. With the
shipped data the bite resists stagger from its first tick through its last
active tick, so its recovery is the punish window; the pounce can be
interrupted early in its wind-up and resists from mid wind-up through its last
active tick. The headless 60 s fight (`out/preview/demon_wolf/ingame/`,
developer evidence) showed:

- player hits inside an immune window: 35, staggers 0; outside: 36, staggers
  20, of which 17 cancelled a move (bites were cancelled in recovery);
- a player who stands still and trades is hit by the pounce and two bites per
  wolf before killing it, about 14 of 20 health on Normal, no deaths;
- in the dodge trial, strafing during the bite wind-up, from move tick 0 or
  from the turn lock, avoided the bite;
- the pounce's measured landing tick and apex agree with the generator's
  flight model; the `moves` landing stills and the `slowmo` video show the
  landing with the one-step lerp (the hanging pose before it is kept in
  `out/preview/demon_wolf/ingame_lerp_before/`).

Stepping away from a pounce after its lock was not measured. Nothing about the
wolf has been observed by the owner on a physical client: its look, how the
fight feels, sound (the build host has no audio device), `F3+T` reload of the
model files, and multiplayer sync are `not_verified`. The README ledger "Demon
Wolf (0.33.0)" records each surface. Offline previews are in
`out/preview/demon_wolf/`.
